The Exam Day Guide

Good revision earns you the marks. A calm, organised exam day makes sure you collect them. Here is the routine, from the evening before to the final minute.

The evening before

Stop learning new material by early evening. Read through your one-page summaries once, then put them away.

  • Pack your bag: pens, pencils, ruler, calculator with fresh batteries, any permitted materials, water and your ID or candidate number.
  • Check the time, the room and how you are getting there.
  • Lay out your clothes. Set two alarms.
  • Go to bed at your normal time. Sleep is when memory is consolidated, so an all-night session costs more than it gives.

The morning

Eat something, even if you are not hungry. Arrive fifteen to twenty minutes early, which is enough to settle and not enough to become anxious. Avoid the group outside the door comparing what they have and have not revised. That conversation never helps anyone.

The first five minutes

When you are told to begin, do not start writing at once.

  1. Read the instructions on the front: how many questions, which are compulsory.
  2. Skim the whole paper so nothing on the last page surprises you.
  3. Mark the questions you feel confident about.
  4. Work out your time per question from the marks available.

Divide your time by marks

Marks tell you how long to spend. Divide the minutes available by the total marks, keep a tenth of the time back for checking, and you have a rate.

Write the finish time for each section in the margin. When you reach it, move on, even mid-sentence. The first marks on a new question are easier to win than the last marks on the old one.

When you get stuck

Everyone meets a question they cannot do. Put a star beside it and move on at once. Your mind keeps working on it in the background, and the answer often surfaces while you are writing something else. If your mind goes blank entirely, put the pen down, breathe slowly out for a count of six, three times, and start with the easiest question on the page.

The last ten minutes

Stop writing new material and check. Have you answered every part of every question? Are units and labels in place? In calculations, does the answer look sensible? Return to starred questions and write something for each; a blank earns nothing, and a reasonable attempt often earns a mark or two.

Afterwards

Do not hold a post-mortem in the corridor. The paper is finished and cannot be changed. Go home, eat, rest for an hour, and then look at what is next on your timetable. The exam you can still affect is the next one.
